Why winter mornings are the worst

  1. Night

    The ground cools fast. Warmer air settles on top like a lid, and smoke from traffic, cooking and burning waste stays trapped underneath.

  2. Morning

    The lid is still low. Everything emitted overnight is packed into a thin layer of air, right where people walk and breathe.

  3. Afternoon

    Sunlight warms the ground, the lid lifts and the same smoke spreads through a much deeper layer. The air at street level is usually at its cleanest.

That is why AETHER reads the forecast mixing height, not just the AQI, when it picks your hours.

For the people you worry about

  • A child with football after school

    Moved to the cleanest hour before dark, or swapped for an indoor game when even that hour is unhealthy.

  • A grandparent who walks every morning

    Winter mornings are often the smokiest hours. AETHER points to a cleaner slot later in the day, or a walk indoors.

  • A runner with asthma

    Hard exercise counts one step stricter, because you breathe several times more air while running. Inhaler reminders included.

  • Someone expecting a baby

    Treated as a sensitive group throughout, with indoor air steps first and outdoor time kept to the cleanest hours.

How it decides

  1. You tell it who is at home

    A name or role and any sensitivities for each person, plus today’s plans. Names are not sent to the AI.

  2. It reads the air

    The next 24 hours of the Copernicus CAMS forecast, and the nearest reference monitor, such as a CPCB station, where one has reported recently.

  3. Published rules decide

    EPA AQI categories and health guidance set every verdict, the same way every time. No model is in the loop.

  4. Gemini explains

    Google Gemini turns the finished plan into a short briefing in plain words. It cannot change a verdict or a number.

Forecast and measurement, side by side

Forecast

A model estimate from Copernicus CAMS on a regional grid, for the next 24 hours. It covers places with no monitor nearby, so it drives the planning. Always labelled as an estimate.

Measured

The nearest official monitor within 25 km that has reported in the last day, exactly as its operator publishes it, with its distance and age. Shown beside the forecast, never blended into it.

Anything older than three hours is marked stale. Missing readings stay missing.

Questions people ask

Is this medical advice?

No. AETHER turns public air-quality data and published guidance into a daily plan. Follow your doctor’s advice and your local health authority’s alerts, and seek care for chest pain or severe breathlessness.

Where do the numbers come from?

Forecasts come from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service through Open-Meteo. Station readings come from OpenAQ, which carries government networks such as India’s CPCB. Google Gemini only writes the briefing text.

What happens to my household details?

They are saved in your browser so you don’t have to retype them. To build a plan they are sent to AETHER’s server, kept in memory for at most five minutes to write the briefing, and never written to disk or logged. Avoid putting names in the free-text note, because that note is passed to the AI.

Does it work outside India?

Yes. The CAMS forecast covers the whole world. The station card appears wherever OpenAQ has a monitor within 25 km that has reported in the last day.

Why can the forecast and the station disagree?

A station measures one spot, and a busy road or a nearby fire can push it up or down. The forecast estimates an average over a wider area. AETHER shows both so you can judge, and plans with the forecast because it looks ahead.
